What this map can and cannot tell you

This is the Lake Victoria regional research preview: one window around Lake Victoria (2°S to 3°N, 30°E to 36°E), built in WQL-002. It shows a few openly licensed observations on a common hexagonal grid. It is not a measure of quality of life, and it does not say where life is better or worse.

What it is not

  • Not a score or a ranking. Every layer is shown on its own. Nothing is added up, weighted or ranked, and colours are classes of the values in this window only.
  • Not a measurement at 36 km². The H3 resolution-6 hexagon is a common index for joining sources. Each value says what grid or points it came from and how many fell in the cell.
  • Not a count of people. Both population layers are models that spread census totals over mapped buildings. They disagree with each other, and two layers show by how much: the relative difference (% of their mean) and the absolute difference (people). Read them together.
  • No conflict data in this preview. Cell-level conflict records are withheld. At H3 resolution 6, 83 cells would show a single recorded event with its fatality estimate and range, which does not clear commitment C-11 (do no harm). A coarser conflict release will be reviewed separately.
  • Not a statement about facilities. Travel time to the nearest health facility says nothing about whether that facility has staff, medicine or fees people can pay.
  • Not a boundary authority. Country outlines come from geoBoundaries and are drawn for orientation. Where two outlines overlap the map says so. The short list of contested places is not drawn from a neutral register and is incomplete.

Known gaps in this version

  • Eastern DRC has had no census since at least 1984, so every population figure there rests on a far older anchor than its neighbours'. The census-age layer shows this directly.
  • Some Lake Victoria islands and shoreline settlements fall outside the simplified country outlines, so they have no country-level values even though people live there.
  • Three of the eight core v0.1 layers are not here: night-light radiance (needs a login to the source), PM2.5 (download host not yet allowed in the build environment) and conflict deaths (withheld, above). The evidence-grading rules are still simplified.
  • Population figures are for 2020, travel time for 2019 to 2020, and census dates as of 2026. They are not one moment in time.

How to read the evidence flag

Every value carries six evidence grades from A to D: recency, directness, spatial fidelity, coverage, validation and interval. The badge shows the weakest of the six, as a warning, not as a quality score. Open "Evidence grade by dimension" on any value to see all six, and which were lowered for that particular cell.

Missing means missing

Hatched cells have no value for the layer shown. The map never fills a gap with a guess, and the inspector says why each gap exists.

Corrections

If something here is wrong about a place you know, please open an issue at github.com/nomenolly/worldwide-quality-of-life/issues. A fuller channel for affected people to challenge observations (C-14) is planned but not built.

Boundary-derived data (ODbL 1.0). The country outlines, each cell's country coverage, the boundary-overlap and dispute flags, years since census per cell, and the population recency grade that depends on it all derive from geoBoundaries outlines. The Uganda and DRC outlines are under ODbL 1.0, so this whole package is offered under ODbL 1.0. If you publicly use an adapted version, you must offer it under ODbL too, and keep it free of technical restrictions.
